Where we apply something because we know it works, but there's no underlying theory.

Examples include where doctors will prescribe drugs without the research actually demonstrating _why_ it works. See also AI models from neural networks through to Large Language Models (LLMs) which work by the same means.

We know that correlation does not mean causation, but correlation somehow appears uncomfortably useful even without an underlying theory.

Intuitively, this makes sense. If a drug or treatment makes you better, you don't really care why so long as you get the results.

Similarly, this is how people are applying AI: it does what I want, so it doesn't matter if it doesn't have understanding or theory. Except when it goes wrong.

I think there is the limit of AI-assisted coding. It will never have an underlying theory of the archictecture, design or even the business model. It can only do this that logically follow from language that _describes_ those things. This appears to work a lot of the time with enough context engineering, but there's no theory so it risks diverging from what you want without the right management.
