It is my belief that more people want regulation and rules than they can even admit to themselves. I think we rely heavily on regulation and accountability or else certain systems crumble.

The disappointing second part to this observation is that when it is working, nobody acknowledges that is it working.

That is, we somehow reach a point where we willing hand our hard-earned money over to a bank but not realise that is only possible because should they simply refuse to give it back, there's consequences for the bank and its shareholders. They don't give it back out of good will.

The clear consequence of this is that people only grate up against regulation when it holds them back, when it frustrates them.

The conclusion of this theory is therefore that we can explain why large proportions of the population can be convinced that regulation is intrinsically bad, that it has "gone too far", that is is holding back growth all while clearly benefitting from it daily. There is no contradiction in the mind if those benefits are simply not seen.

Thus we seem examples of regulation being cut back and being being truly harmed. Companies routinely shift to "gig economy" workers over employees to bypass employment regulation and those workers suffer.
